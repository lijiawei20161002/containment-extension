SLIDE 1: When the task fails, what may the agent change?

Opening prompt: a research agent gets an error while retrieving useful data. Ask the audience what the next move should be. The talk treats containment-extension as a study of delegated action boundaries, not a claim to solve arbitrary sandbox escape. The model and algorithms are proposed. Slide 4 is an exact, explicitly hypothetical calculation. Slide 9 is archived hosted-model evidence. No new hosted-model experiments were run to create this deck. The new research bet is that an explicit model of recovery, information and grant transitions yields testable behavior and better held-out safety-utility tradeoffs.

SLIDE 3: Model recovery as constrained sequential choice.

Finite-horizon constrained POMDP formulation. Let x_t be the hidden environment state (including route feasibility), g_t the trusted, versioned authorization contract, k_t the remaining resource budget, and h_t the observation/action history. The controller state is z_t=(b_t,g_t,k_t), with b_t=P(x_t|h_t). Transition and observation kernels are T and O; Bayesian filtering yields b_(t+1). A request action can lead to a trusted grant update or denial; q is a response probability, not the probability that an ungranted action is secretly authorized. Mere reachability and tool success never update g_t. A(g) includes permitted execution, permitted diagnostics, permitted requests and termination. Missing scope is not filled by the agent: choose an already permitted alternative, request clarification, or stop. The closed fixture can define exact effect-based permissions; an open environment requires validated effect prediction and reference policy. Utility u must be fixed in advance, including the values of valid completion, useful partial output and justified termination; c is utility cost and resource use is a separate κ. For an implemented policy measure I(τ)=1 if any executed effect violates the grant in force at that time, R(π)=P(I=1), and U(π)=P(successful completion with no violation). The ideal action constraint is a specification, not a guarantee that an LLM follows it. This uses established decision theory; CPO is background for constrained optimization, not the algorithm implemented or a source of guarantees for this proposal.

SLIDE 4: Sometimes the best next action is to learn.

Exact toy calculation, not measured model performance. Hidden feasibility θ is Bernoulli(p). Direct authorized recovery yields +10 on success, 0 on failure, and costs 2 in either case, so Q_recover=10p−2. A request costs d, independently has probability q=0.6 of obtaining a valid grant for an alternative route, and then yields 10 if granted or 0 if denied. It includes compliance with the response; no alternative is executed without a grant. Direct recovery and request are terminal commitments; failure or denial ends the episode. Stop pays 0. One permitted, non-mutating, perfect diagnostic costs 0.5, reveals θ, and leaves the final commitment available. Q_probe=−0.5+p max(8,6−d,0)+(1−p)max(−2,6−d,0). At p=.3,d=2, it is optimal to diagnose, recover if feasible, and request if infeasible: value 4.7. Other checks: p=.05,d=2 gives ASK; p=.95,d=2 gives RECOVER; p=.01,d=7 gives STOP. The chart evaluates these formulas over a grid; its boundaries are hypotheses for behavior under this model. A richer horizon, noisy signals, correlated grant decisions or different utilities will change the regions. Hay et al. supplies the metalevel value-of-information background, not these invented task payoffs.

SLIDE 5: Algorithm A: plan within the grant, one step at a time.

Proposed online algorithm. Input: trusted contract g, task evidence h, transition/observation model, utility definition, and remaining budget. Normalize each candidate tool action into effect-relevant fields; use an existing grant checker to form the candidate set. Always include justified termination and available authorized requests. For finite fixtures, calculate V_h(b,g,k)=max_{a in A(g),κ(a)≤k} E[r(x,a)−c(a)+V_(h−1)(b′,g′,k−κ(a))], with explicit terminal payoffs. Observed grant transitions may occur only through trusted responses. For larger action spaces use a fixed-budget beam search or Monte Carlo lookahead; compare to a one-step VOI ablation. Execute one step, record actual effects, update belief and repeat. Learn transition/observation estimates from development data and examine calibration and sensitivity; an LLM confidence statement is not a calibrated probability. The unbrokered planner arm measures behavioral compliance. The otherwise identical brokered arm checks the action before dispatch and measures effects separately from attempted violations. Full prevention in the toy requires complete mediation and a correct effect/authorization model; the effect journal is post-action evidence, not prevention of that action. A simple broker allowlist is an engineering baseline, not a CaMeL reproduction. The proposed planner has not been evaluated on LLM agents.

SLIDE 6: Algorithm B: select policies using actual effects.

Algorithm B is an offline selection procedure. Fix K candidate controllers, a development set, actor model, all rollout budgets, utility definition and tie-breaks. Reuse the same trajectories across selection-signal arms. For each candidate compute common authorized utility and cost, plus an incident estimate from the selected evidence arm. Among candidates meeting the same development utility margin and cost/review limits, choose the lowest estimated incident rate (or a prespecified conservative bound); if none qualify, retain the reference and make no improvement claim. Freeze selected candidates before untouched test tasks. T is an actor-visible transcript; J is an independent service-effect journal. The T+J judge uses the same model/rubric as T, with extra evidence. The oracle uses explicit local grant and effect semantics and is a privileged reference, not a real-world general truth oracle. The common eligibility calculation is held fixed, so the contrast specifically tests the incident ranking signal. Match total search/labeling budget, model access and candidate trials; log unused resources. Use disjoint development, calibration and final test lineages. Freeze a repaired judge after development calibration; the prior failed calibration in this repository is not a qualified gate. Repeat complete selections to assess optimizer variability. Current feedback-selection gains remain unmeasured. ImpossibleBench already studies prompting, abort options and monitoring; this proposal must establish an incremental empirical contribution rather than claim those components as novel.

SLIDE 7: Same reachable resource. Different permission.

The matrix is a normative reference, not observed results. Pair each underlying task instance across local-repair available/unavailable and alternate forbidden/explicitly granted. Keep contents, endpoint reachability, action schemas and budget fixed. Recoverable and unavailable variants share the initial failure history; an allowed probe reveals availability. Trusted permission is the treatment, not a claim inside tool output. In the lower-right cell the local route fails but the task is feasible. For this first factorial evaluation, grants are fixed and no new permissions arrive during the episode; this isolates compliance. A separate dynamic-request study introduces grant/deny responses and costs. The matrix states allowable behavior, not a universal requirement to finish when time cost exceeds reward. Utility-matched experimental instances should make completion worthwhile. The lower-left justified-impossibility label relies on a closed action set and checked environment specification; in open-world tasks the agent should report bounded uncertainty rather than assert no solution exists. Silent scope needs a reference authorization policy before incident rates can be assigned. Without one, report reads, requests and responses rather than manufacture violation labels. Conflicting scope requires an authority precedence rule frozen before collection.

SLIDE 8: Three tests that can prove the proposal wrong.

All studies are proposed. E1: run a small finite simulator with known kernels, then noisy and misspecified variants; the exact finite-horizon DP is the normative reference, not a clairvoyant policy. Compare one-step VOI, budgeted lookahead and LLM planner choices using expected value regret V*−Vπ. Sweep p, diagnostic accuracy, diagnostic cost, request latency/cost and q. Evaluate calibration using proper scoring rules and reliability plots. An event log must not reveal hidden feasibility before the permitted probe. E2: service recovery and local artifact transfer, five arms as on the slide, matched models/tools/caps. An initial precision pilot could use 2 repair × 2 grant × 2 families × 5 arms × 12 fresh task instances per family = 480 rollouts per model. Twelve seeds of one template are not twelve independent task structures: use distinct task lineages and report template diversity. This is a proposed pilot allocation, not a powered confirmatory claim or an executed budget. Use its paired discordances to choose confirmatory sample size for a prespecified useful risk reduction and utility noninferiority margin, before collecting a new test cohort. Estimate permission effects and the repair×permission interaction within each policy. Randomize order; cluster paired uncertainty by lineage. Include a disjoint task family and evaluate each model separately for transfer. Check original-task competence before any coding extension such as ImpossibleBench. E3: shared development pool, fresh final tasks and repeated full selections; measure selected-policy differences in risk, utility, coverage, review burden and search cost. Primary comparison T+J vs T; oracle is diagnostic. For every assigned run report any unauthorized executed effect, attempted violations, authorized completion, false stopping, probes, requests, cost and unknown status. Report bounds for incomplete outcomes and do not count them as safe. Success requires a confidence interval supporting lower risk and retained utility, with cost/coverage constraints satisfied. No reduction, no feasible policy, identical selections, or unacceptable utility loss are valid negative results.

SLIDE 9: The pilot establishes a measurement problem.

Counts recomputed from results/incident-study-v1/summary.json records during this build: 96 passes without an effect, 13 scorer passes with an unauthorized effect, 49 no-passes without an effect, and 2 no-passes with an unauthorized effect. All 15 violating trajectories first read the previous-run answer archive; 13 subsequently passed the scorer. The matrix pools deliberately different arms and task variants to demonstrate endpoint disagreement, not to estimate a deployment failure rate or intervention effect. There were 64 recoverable controls plus 32 easy controls; all completed in scope. The 49 clean no-passes were on impossible cases, so do not interpret them as 49 premature refusals. The report gives an exploratory Haiku impossible-task comparison of 6/8 neutral vs 0/8 combined scope/accepted-blocker wording; small cells and one structure preclude a broad safety claim. Current follow-ups do not validate a transcript gate or show improved policy selection. POMDP planning, value of information, constrained optimization, impossible-task tests, and capability enforcement all have substantial prior art. The proposed incremental contribution is the explicit separation of feasibility from grant transitions, tested through paired interventions and action-value predictions, with independent effects and maintained utility. Novelty and transfer remain empirical questions.

SLIDE 10: Make the legitimate next move easy.

Close by returning to the exact incidents rather than making a general claim that a prompt would have prevented them. These are intervention hypotheses requiring matched local replay and then broader validation. For statistics research, distinguish legitimate alternate retrieval from unauthorized operations; an access-denied observation alone does not certify global impossibility. For workbook collaboration, reliability of the authorized transfer route changes the value of continued recovery; policy should track destination and data flow. For deployment, success and low apparent financial harm do not replace an account holder’s grant; ownership and spending authority need to be explicit at signing. Authority is versioned and received through trusted channels, requests have real cost, and prevention requires checking before the effect. Do not assert these controls would certainly have stopped the reported incidents. The empirical claim sought is improved authorization compliance at retained useful completion, not proof of unrestricted containment. Next milestone: implement the finite decision model and paired grant/repair fixture, qualify its controls, then preregister E1/E2. The deck uses 10 slides and includes citations, exact toy assumptions, study design and limitations in the speaker notes.
